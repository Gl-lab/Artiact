## Context

Epic 5 follows the bounded minimum of roadmap Epic 4, not every historical mining ambition. Inventory remediation and real-world map access remain unresolved. The current pipeline stays intact during research.

Source inspection at `4b49b1e` shows:

- `TargetLootingResolver` accepts monsters up to character level + 1 without HP/equipment viability.
- `StepBuilder.BuildLootingSteps` limits fights to ten, but has no rest or defeat recovery.
- `CraftTargetEvaluator` orders by item level while ignoring character stats.
- `LevelUpGoal` permits a null target and is not handled by decomposition/building.
- `GameClient` exposes Fight/Rest/EquipItem/UnequipItem through a shared ActionResponse. Its mutating requests can be retried after ambiguous failures; methods are tokenless.
- MockService only supplies mining scenarios. A passing mock suite does not prove combat compatibility.

See [planning source observations](source-observations.md) for upstream anchors and preliminary schema gaps.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** resolve roadmap D6; identify a minimum defensible viability rule, recovery policy, goal vocabulary and API subset; prove finite offline transitions; produce an explicit Epic 6 go/no-go decision.

**Non-goals:** production integration, a universal simulator, tuning against a real character, selecting best gear from tracked caches, or changing any existing mining/loot behavior.

## Decisions

### 1. Evidence precedes model selection

Create `docs/research/combat-equipment/contract-matrix.md`. For each required route record method/path, auth/access, request and response schema pointers, required/null fields, errors and cooldown, local symbol, compatibility status, source retrieval date/version and remaining uncertainty. Cover fight/rest/equip/unequip, character, maps/movement, monsters, items/effects and optional simulation. Include crafting only where the candidate equipment prerequisite needs it. Distinguish source-verified schema, documented mechanics, synthetic assumptions and runtime observations. Revalidate initial observations during execution; record a public OpenAPI content hash without checking in the full downloaded document.

Create `mechanics.md` covering elemental/global damage, rounding, resistance, critical randomness, initiative ties, effects, HP, turn limit, defeat, XP, drops, slot conditions, stat changes and recovery. Missing or contradictory mechanics remain unknown with a named blocking impact, never silently inferred from DTO defaults.

### 2. Compare bounded alternatives without committing to one

Evaluate a conservative local predictor, optional official simulator evidence and observed-outcome calibration on the same named fixtures. Compare required inputs, uncertainty coverage, false-safe cases, explainability, offline reproducibility and maintenance cost. Official simulation must not become a membership/network dependency. Calibration uses only public or synthetic evidence in this scope; collecting private/live fights is not required. If no model can justify even a narrow subset, a no-go is a valid research outcome.

Start candidate scope with one character, normal monsters and explicit supported effects/equipment. Unsupported effects, uncertain access and missing stats yield Unknown. Do not translate average win rate into a safety guarantee. The ADR must state the chosen risk criterion and numeric boundaries with evidence; any unresolved operator risk preference remains an Epic 6 blocker. A conservative supported-subset model is a hypothesis, not a preselected result.

### 3. Isolate executable experiments

Use a standalone .NET 9 xUnit experiment project under `experiments/`, excluded from Artiact.sln and production references. Read relevant nested instructions before any later project edits. Use in-memory scripted responses, virtual cooldowns and a recording action port; no production GameClient retry path, credentials, network, wall-clock sleeps or hosted worker. Author only the small models needed to test hypotheses. Retain minimal reviewable source/fixtures and commands as evidence; do not check in bin/obj, logs or generated traces. Never promote prototype types to production without Epic 6 design review.

Test pure prediction separately from orchestration. Expected results must be independently calculated or source-grounded, not generated by the predictor. Repeat every deterministic fixture and compare decisions, action count, returned state and virtual duration. Stochastic hypotheses use fixed inputs/seeds and explicitly report uncertainty; sample success alone is not a proof of safety.

### 4. Define goal and transition semantics explicitly

Candidate milestone: reach a positive configured combat level using authoritative character level/XP. Decide whether LevelUpGoal should be clarified or replaced; specify null/invalid/already-complete targets and precedence. Equipment and recovery are prerequisites, not extra unbounded objectives. Compare equipment against current loadout and selected opponent; a higher item level alone is insufficient.

Each prototype decision emits at most one simulated mutating command, then reconciles and reevaluates. This is an experiment rule, not a change to current mining's Move-plus-Gather bound. Configure positive decision/fight/recovery/no-progress limits before running; reject invalid limits. Charge attempts before dispatch, do not refund on failure, and include recovery/equipment loops in the total limit.

Specify Completed, Selected and Blocked with stable reason/facts; model unsafe, unknown, unreachable, inventory pressure, invalid state, defeat, recovery failure, exhausted budget and no progress. Define counter reset and progress measures so rest/equip oscillations cannot masquerade as combat XP progress. Stop at target without further mutation. Save returned state before cancellation; changed HP, location, inventory and equipment override predictions. Ambiguous response loss permits no retry or subsequent mutating action; end Blocked/unknown outcome and document reconciliation required for future execution. Do not claim this fixes production transport.

### 5. Decision package is the exit gate

Write `docs/decisions/0001-combat-viability-and-recovery.md` (choose the next free number if necessary) with alternatives, evidence links, supported/excluded mechanics, risk boundaries, recovery/defeat/unknown-outcome policy and full-emulator rationale. A go recommendation must name starting state, target, supported monster/equipment tier, minimum mock routes, DTO/client migrations and executable Epic 6 acceptance scenarios. A no-go must identify missing evidence and the next bounded investigation. Explain restart policy; in-process bounds do not imply durable recovery.

## Required experiment cases

| Group | Required examples and independent observations |
|---|---|
| Viability | Safe, unsafe, exact HP/rounding boundary, initiative tie, critical uncertainty, unsupported effect, missing/invalid stats, zero damage and turn exhaustion |
| Milestone | Already complete, valid progression, invalid target/XP, unchanged XP, decision/fight budget exhaustion |
| Recovery | Low HP with successful rest; rest returns unchanged/invalid HP; recovery failure/budget; defeat with authoritative relocation; no automatic revenge loop |
| Equipment | Useful lower-level item versus worse higher-level item, unmet condition, invalid slot, insufficient inventory, failed swap and stat changes requiring reevaluation |
| Execution | Unreachable/changed destination, stale preconditions, full inventory, cancellation before dispatch and after response, lost response with exactly one dispatch and no replay |
| Replay | Identical scripted inputs produce identical terminal reason, ordered commands, state and virtual cooldown total |

## Risks / Trade-offs

- Upstream schema and mechanics drift: dated anchors and recheck at research completion; contradictions block affected claims.
- Simplified fixtures can hide danger: use independent boundary oracles and explicitly excluded mechanics.
- Legacy DTO defaults can mask shape changes: experiment deserialization against authored schema-shaped responses and distinguish transport success from valid character reconciliation.
- Existing retry and map-access gaps: identify them as Epic 6 prerequisites, not silently solved by an offline action port.
- Research can grow indefinitely: restrict to one supported normal-monster tier and one equipment comparison; report no-go instead of building a general emulator.
